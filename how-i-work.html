How I Work

What to expect when I join your team

Every business I walk into thinks it knows what the customer problem is. Sometimes they are right. Often they are partly right. Occasionally they are solving the wrong problem entirely. My job is to find out which one it is before we spend money fixing anything.

01

Understand what the business believes the problem is

I start by listening. What does leadership think is going wrong? What does the frontline team see? What has already been tried? I want to understand the story the business is telling itself about its customers before I look at any data.

02

Audit the data behind that belief

Then I look at what the business already knows. Survey results, NPS scores, complaint logs, call recordings, CRM data — most companies have more customer data than they realise. The question is whether anyone has brought it together into a single picture.

03

Bring in what the business does not see

Customers do not only talk to you. They leave reviews on Hellopeter. They complain on social media. They behave in ways your internal data does not capture. I pull in external signals — public reviews, competitor benchmarks, behavioural data — to fill the gaps.

04

Build a single view of customer truth

I consolidate everything — complaints, surveys, reviews, behavioural signals — into one view. This is usually where the real problems become visible, because no single data source tells the full story. The pattern only shows up when you put them side by side.

05

Identify root causes and design interventions

With a clear picture, I identify what is actually causing the problem — not the symptoms, but the root. Then I design structured changes: to journeys, to processes, to how teams communicate with customers. Every intervention ties back to a business metric, not just a satisfaction score.

06

Make it stick

The hardest part of CX work is not the diagnosis or the design. It is making sure the changes hold after the project ends. I embed CX thinking into how teams operate, so improvements do not depend on one person pushing them forward.

The goal

Turn customer insight into action that improves both the experience and the business outcome. If a change only helps the customer but does not help the business, it will not survive. If it only helps the business but makes the customer's life harder, it should not survive.
