How I Work

I've held Head of Customer Experience roles at Absa and the ooba Home Loans group. In both, I was brought in to answer the same question: is what we think about our customers the truth?

The method 01Listen 02Learn 03Act 04Needs meet reality 05Bringing it together
01

Listen

I start with two conversations.

The first is with the business. What does leadership believe is going wrong? What does the frontline team see? What has already been tried? I want to understand the story the business tells itself about its customers before I look at any data.

The second conversation is with the customer. Not just what internal reporting shows, but what external signals reveal. Most businesses have more customer data than they realise. The task is to bring everything together to show the full picture. At ooba, for example, that meant building a measurement framework that let group leadership compare customer experience across home loans, insurance and new-business incubation for the first time. We surveyed roughly 1,100 customers a month across eight journeys, giving leadership the view they needed.

02

Learn

When I put both views side by side, the gaps show up quickly. What the business thinks the customer is experiencing and what the customer is really experiencing are often not the same thing.

Those gaps are where the value sits. The same insight that tells me where customers drop off also tells me what to say to keep them. At oobainsure, the question was what promise to lead with: lower premiums or quick claims turnaround? When I listened to almost 18,000 customer voices, the answer was neither. Customers wanted clarity: insurance messaging that was easy to understand. That insight shaped the experience redesign and the brand positioning at the same time.

customer voices analysed at oobainsure. The answer they gave was clarity — not price, not speed.

03

Act

Insights might reshape the customer journey, the brand positioning, or both. Every change has to improve the customer's experience in a way the business can measure.

Making that measurable means the people delivering the experience need to be involved in designing the change. I bring frontline teams and stakeholders into the process early, because a solution built with the people accountable for it is one they'll defend when priorities shift.

When I designed a new lead qualification model for ooba's home loan prequalification, I didn't replace the existing process outright. Instead, I ran both in parallel for eight months so the teams working the leads could see the difference in their own numbers. The new model converted at five times the rate, and by the time leadership made the call, the change was already underway.

04

Where customer needs meet organisational reality

Good customer insight helps you make better decisions. It doesn't remove the uncertainty that comes with making them.

What didn't land · ooba

Home WiFi

At ooba, customer research showed demand for a home WiFi product for people moving into new homes. We launched it and it didn't land. What customers say they want and what they actually do can differ.

What didn't land · Absa

Repayment reward savings

At Absa, I proposed rewarding personal loan customers who never missed a repayment by redirecting their instalment into a savings account once the loan was settled. The idea made sense for the customer, but the savings account sat in a different business unit and the two couldn't work together.

Both taught me the same thing: customer insight that hasn't been tested against operational reality and actual behaviour isn't ready for investment yet.

05

How I bring it together

That's how I work now. Customer research sits alongside behavioural evidence, and good ideas get tested against operational reality before they get budget. I bring CX and marketing together, helping organisations understand what customers need, decide what that means for the experience and the brand, and build the internal alignment that makes the change last.
